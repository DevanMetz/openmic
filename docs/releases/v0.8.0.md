OpenMic 0.8.0 is a new layout: a sidebar app for setting up and tuning, which collapses into a thin strip for everyday use.

## Sidebar

- Five pages: **Voice** (presets, noise reduction, the live scope and mixer, the headphone monitor and **Test my mic**), **Soundboard**, **Record**, **Dictation**, and **Settings** (routing and devices, hotkeys, startup, updates and logs).
- A status panel at the bottom of the sidebar stays on every page: whether Discord hears you, your output level, **Mute**, **Start/Stop**, and **Collapse to strip**.
- The Voice page shows where your voice goes in one line (**Change** opens the devices under Settings) and flags a missing VB-Cable or wrong output right there.

## The strip

- **Collapse to strip** swaps the window for a thin always-on-top bar: click the microphone to mute, see your level, click the preset to switch to the next one, play your starred pads, and click ⛶ (or double-click) to expand again.
- Star a pad on the Soundboard page to put it on the strip.
- Drag the strip's right edge to resize it. Narrower, it drops pads, then the preset, then the bar, down to just the microphone, which replaces the old pinned meter.
- OpenMic reopens the way you left it: as the strip or as the window. Windows startup still starts in the notification area.

## Fixes

- Several symbols (checklist ticks, the mic test's play buttons, the recording dot, the update dismiss button, the downloaded-model mark) drew as empty boxes because the built-in fonts don't have them. They now use symbols that exist, and a test checks every symbol in the interface.

## Download and upgrade

Click the version number (or wait for the daily check), then **Update** and **Restart to update**. Or download `openmic-v0.8.0-windows-x64.exe` and run it in place of the previous version. If you had pinned the level meter, use **Collapse to strip** instead.

Requires Windows 10 or 11 (x64) and the [Microsoft Visual C++ v14 Redistributable (x64)](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/cpp/windows/latest-supported-vc-redist). The `.sha256` and `.sig` assets hold the executable's checksum and signature.
